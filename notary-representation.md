## Frequently asked questions about notary representation in Spain

### Is a Spanish notary the same as a notary in my country?

No, and this is one of the most common misunderstandings. In most English-speaking countries, a notary simply witnesses a signature. In Spain, a notary is a public official who verifies the legality of the whole transaction, checks the identity of everyone involved, and explains the document before it's signed. That's why a Spanish notary asks more questions and takes more time than the notary you might be used to.

### Do I have to be in Spain to sign at the notary?

No. With a power of attorney, someone can sign on your behalf. Most of our clients never attend the signing in person.

### What's the difference between a specific and a general power of attorney?

A specific power of attorney limits your representative to exactly what's written in it, for example, signing one particular purchase. A general power of attorney gives much broader authority and carries more risk if it's not carefully worded. For a single transaction, a specific power of attorney is almost always the safer choice.

### Can I sign the power of attorney in my own country?

Yes. You can sign it at a Spanish consulate near you, which is accepted in Spain without an apostille, or before a local notary in your country, which does need an apostille plus a sworn translation if it isn't in Spanish.

### What is an apostille and do I need one?

An apostille is an official stamp that certifies a document signed abroad is genuine, so it can be used in Spain. You need one if you sign the power of attorney before a local notary in your country. You don't need one if you sign it directly at a Spanish consulate.

### How long does arranging a power of attorney take?

At a Spanish consulate, it can often be arranged within a few days. Abroad, with an apostille and a sworn translation, it typically takes longer — from a few days to a few weeks depending on your country. We tell you what to expect once we know where you're signing.

### Can I revoke a power of attorney once it's granted?

Yes, at any time. Revoking it has to be done before a notary, and your representative needs to be formally notified so they stop using it.
