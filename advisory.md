## Frequently asked questions about advisory

### Can a foreigner set up a company in Spain?

Yes, there are no nationality restrictions. You'll need a NIE (Número de Identificación de Extranjero) and a Spanish bank account. We handle both as part of the incorporation process. The whole thing typically takes two to four weeks.

### Autónomo or SL: which is right for me?

An autónomo is simpler and cheaper to set up, but gives you no separation between personal and business liability. An SL costs more to incorporate and has higher administrative overhead, but protects your personal assets and is more efficient for larger turnover or multiple shareholders. The right answer depends on your situation: that's what the first consultation is for.

### Do I need to be physically in Spain to incorporate a company?

No. With a notarised power of attorney, we manage the full process remotely. The only step that sometimes requires your presence is opening a corporate bank account — though this is also possible remotely with some banks.

### What taxes does a Spanish SL pay?

The main ones are Impuesto sobre Sociedades (25% on annual profits for standard companies; 23% for smaller businesses under €10M turnover; 15% for newly incorporated companies in their first two profitable years), IVA (VAT, 21% standard rate, filed quarterly), and social security contributions for any employees. We manage all of it.

### Can you draft contracts in English?

Yes — we draft bilingual contracts in English and Spanish where required. The Spanish version is the legally operative one, but you receive both so you understand exactly what you're signing.

### How much does company formation cost in Spain?

Total costs — notary, Mercantile Registry and our fees — typically range between €1,500 and €3,500 depending on complexity. We give you a fixed quote before starting. No surprises.

### How long does it take to set up a company in Spain?

For a standard SL, the process takes between two and four weeks from the moment we have everything in place: NIE, bank account, signed power of attorney and agreed articles of association. The main variable is the notary and Mercantile Registry turnaround, which can run faster or slower depending on the province and time of year. We tell you at the start what to expect and flag anything that might cause a delay before it does.
