## Frequently asked questions about the NIE in Spain

### Is the NIE the same as Spanish residency?

No. The NIE is simply an identification number for anyone with economic, professional, or social ties to Spain — it doesn't expire and it doesn't give you the right to live or work here. Residency is a separate process, proven with a physical card called the TIE. You can hold an NIE for years, to own property for example, without ever being a resident.

### Do I need to be in Spain to get an NIE?

No. You can apply in person in Spain, through a Spanish consulate in your home country, or — the option most of our clients choose — by giving us power of attorney, so we apply and collect it on your behalf without you travelling.

### What documents do I need?

Your valid passport, a completed application form (EX-15), a stated reason for the request, and proof of payment of the official fee.

### How long does it take?

By law, the application has to be resolved within 5 working days once formally filed. In practice, appointment availability and office workload affect the real timeline — we tell you what to expect for your specific case rather than a number that doesn't hold up.

### Does the NIE expire?

No, the number itself is permanent. The physical certificate has an issue date, and some institutions prefer a recently issued copy — we can request an updated one if yours is old.

### Can my NIE be used for my spouse or child?

No, an NIE is personal and non-transferable. If other family members also need one — for a joint property purchase, for example — each person applies individually. We can handle all of them together in one process.

### I already started the process myself and it stalled — can you take over?

Yes. We regularly step in when an application was filed with the wrong justification, an incomplete form, or a missed appointment. We review what's already been submitted before restarting anything.
